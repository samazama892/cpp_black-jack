# Splitting

In blackjack, splitting is a strategic option that allows you to turn a single pair of cards into two separate, independent hands. This rule can help you turn a inherently weak starting hand into two potentially winning positions against the dealer.

## How Splitting Works

* **Trigger**: You must be dealt two initial cards of identical value, such as a pair of 8s, 6s, or Aces.
* **Wager**: To split, you must place a second bet next to your original wager. This second bet must exactly equal the amount of your starting stake.
* **Action**: The dealer physically separates the two cards on the layout. They will deal a second card to your first hand.
* **Playing Hands**: You play out the first hand completely (hitting or standing) before moving to the second hand.
* **_Aces_ Exception**: If you split Aces, most casinos will only deal you exactly one additional card per Ace, and you are forced to stand right after.

## Golden Rules

### Always Split

* **Aces**: A pair of Aces totals a weak 2 or 12. Splitting gives you two separate chances to catch a 10-value card for a strong 21.
* **8s**: A pair of 8s totals a hard 16, which is statistically the worst possible starting hand in blackjack. Splitting breaks up the 16 and lets you start two separate hands with an 8.

### Never Split

* **10s** (including Jacks, Queens, Kings): A pair of 10-value cards equals 20. This is already a highly profitable, winning hand. Breaking it up lowers your statistical win probability.
* **5s**: Two 5s equal a hard 10. You are much better off using this hand to hit or double down to try and hit 20 or 21 with a single extra card.

## Situational Splits (Based on Dealer Upcard)

For other pairs, whether you split depends heavily on what card the dealer is showing:

* **9s**: Split if the dealer shows a 2 through 9, except if they show a 7 (against a 7, stand, because your 18 already beats a dealer's predicted 17).
* **2s**, **3s**, and **7s**: Split if the dealer is showing a weak card between 2 and 7; otherwise, just hit.
* **6s**: Split if the dealer shows a 2 through 6; otherwise, hit.
* **4s**: Generally never split them unless the table rules allow "Double After Split" (DAS) and the dealer shows a 5 or 6.
